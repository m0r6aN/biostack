source-graded evidence library.** The enemy seat came at it by naming what BioStack displaces; the
librarian by naming the object; the on-ramp by writing for a first-timer; the skeptic by writing for

They also converged on the **upgrade logic**, which is the part that was previously missing:

> You pay when the question changes — from *"what is this compound"* → *"what about mine"* →

That is exactly the founder's model: the evidence is free, and what you pay for is having it be
about **you**.

**Two exact phrases were independently duplicated across seats**, which is the strongest signal in
the set: *"Every run, side by side"* (enemy + on-ramp, verbatim) and the "free / sourced / graded"
triad (all four).

## ARTIFACT A — the category sentence

### Recommended

> **BioStack is a free, public library of what the research says about peptides and similar compounds — graded by evidence strength, for anyone deciding for themselves.**

Synthesized from the librarian's spine, the skeptic's costly-signal instinct, and the on-ramp's
inclusivity — dropping the on-ramp's own flagged weakness ("your first or your fifteenth" reads
folksy against the clipped house register).

Why this one:

- **"free, public"** front-loads the thing that is actually free, which is the whole positioning
- **"what the research says"** — evidence-first, and legible to someone who's never heard "evidence tier"
- **"graded by evidence strength"** carries the Strong/Moderate/Limited/Mechanistic honesty into the
  sentence. Multiple seats flagged this as the positioning asset: BioStack tells you when the
  evidence is *weak*, which is a costly signal competitors won't make
- **"for anyone deciding for themselves"** does double duty — it satisfies C3 inclusivity with no
  expertise gate, and it is doctrinally perfect: **the user decides, not BioStack.** C1 is satisfied
  inside the sentence rather than by disclaimer
- No safety promise, no storage claim, no invented proof, no banned strings

**One engineering tension, flagged honestly:** at ~161 characters this slightly exceeds the ~155
Google truncates meta descriptions at. Either accept front-loaded truncation (the first clause
carries alone, which it does), or relax the "verbatim in five placements" rule and give meta a
trimmed variant. My brief imposed that rule; it may have been over-tight.

### Runners-up worth keeping on file
- **Skeptic A1** — *"…rates peptide and compound evidence by strength, **not by who's selling it**"*
  — the strongest single differentiator any seat produced. Consider it for the trust section rather
  than the category sentence.
- **Enemy A2** — *"Most people start with a forum thread, a video, or a friend who swears by it…"* —
  best beginner-recognition opener on file. Strong candidate for a section lede.

## ARTIFACT B — the hero third beat

> **"No prescriptions. No guesswork. Just ______."**

| Completion | Seats | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **"Just what's known."** | 2 | **My recommendation.** Mirrors the negation cadence, and lands on epistemic humility — which *is* the brand. Carries the uncertainty markers into the headline: it promises the state of the literature *including its limits*, so it cannot be read as a safety or outcome claim. Strongest C2 fit in the set. |
| **"Just the evidence."** | 3 | **Safest choice, most legible.** Plainest noun, no unpacking required, widest seat support. If you want zero risk, this is it. |
| "Just sources." | 3 | Tightest rhythm match to "Just structure." Promises something checkable. Slightly abstract for a first-timer. |
| "Just receipts." | 1 | Most distinctive; borrows the skeptic's own demand. High risk — reads internet-native in a way that may age badly on a health-adjacent page. |

Between the top two: **"the evidence"** is the safer, clearer pick and had broader support.
**"what's known"** is the better line, because admitting the limits of the literature is the exact
thing that separates you from the people you're positioning against. Your call — I'd ship
*"what's known"* and I'd understand shipping *"the evidence."*

**Consequence either way:** this line currently closes `/safety`. Either give that page a new closer
or make the repetition a deliberate echo, the way "BioStack is not a doctor" already is.

## ARTIFACT C — the tier ladder

Taglines grafted from the enemy and on-ramp seats. *"Every run, side by side"* was produced
verbatim by two independent seats.

| Tier | Price | Tagline | One line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Observer** | **$0** | *The evidence, open.* | The full sourced compound library with evidence grades, source types and mechanism summaries, plus the reconstitution, dose-volume and conversion calculators. No account. Nothing held back. |
| **Operator** | **$12/mo** | *Your protocol, not compounds in general.* | Daily check-ins across ~15 signals, your own timeline, and a flag with supporting evidence when two compounds you're taking share a biological pathway. **You upgrade when you stop reading about compounds and start running specific ones.** |
| **Commander** | **$29/mo** | *Every run, side by side.* | Past protocols and check-ins on one timeline so you can read across them. **You upgrade when you have more than one run worth comparing.** |

`"Longitudinal Intelligence"` → `"Every run, side by side."` Same tier, same features, and now a
newcomer knows what it's for.

Publishing these three prices closes **F5**. Note what the ladder does *not* say: nothing is
withheld from the free tier's evidence, which is the honest expression of "tracking and
personalization are paid side effects."
