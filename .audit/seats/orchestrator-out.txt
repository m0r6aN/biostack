VERDICT: REVISE AND RE-REVIEW

TOP 6 BRUTAL FINDINGS
1. [BLOCKER] The page never says what BioStack is — the only line naming the category is the unstyled `<p>` eyebrow at LandingHero.tsx:79, rendering at 16px body default beneath a 7xl H1 whose three clauses ("What you're taking. How it's structured. See what it's doing.") contain no noun for the product — a first-time visitor completes the 5-second test with no category, so every downstream section is spent on an audience that doesn't know what it's looking at.
2. [BLOCKER] The visually loudest path on the page is a locked door — the only two solid-emerald filled CTAs (proof-section "Analyze a stack", mobile sticky "Analyze") both resolve to /tools/analyzer, which the hero itself labels "Operator required" / "Operator access", while Observer/Operator/Commander are never defined or priced on-page — the page's dominant conversion path terminates in an unexplained paywall.
3. [MAJOR] The four-path IA is structurally false — cards 1 ("Analyze a protocol") and 3 ("Analyze My Stack") sit adjacent in the same row and resolve to the identical URL, and /tools/analyzer is the target of 4 separate controls — a "choose your path" architecture whose paths are not distinct is a false affordance that costs the visitor a real decision for no information gain.
4. [MAJOR] The primary action inverts across breakpoints — desktop color-privileges "Start Free" → /start, while the mobile sticky bar omits /start entirely and color-privileges "Analyze" → /tools/analyzer — the product asks for two different commitments depending on device, so no single primary action exists to optimize or measure.
5. [MAJOR] Zero credibility surface on a health-adjacent product handling sensitive personal data — no proof, no privacy statement, no team, no counts, no dates; the sole trust element is a negative one ("BioStack is not a doctor") placed last — and the answers that would fix it ("Where is my data stored?", "How is BioStack different from a spreadsheet?") are already written in marketing.ts and simply never rendered.
6. [MAJOR] Accessibility falls below the enterprise bar in five independent ways — text-white/35 (~3.1:1) and text-white/42 (~4.0:1) fail AA outright, footer text sits at exactly 4.50:1 with zero margin, focus-visible styling exists on only 4 of ~18 interactive controls, there is no skip link, the panel's role="tablist" has no tabpanel/aria-controls/arrow-key handling, and the auto-rotating insight has no pause control (WCAG 2.2.2).

TOP 5 MOVES
1. Rewrite the hero's top two lines so category and audience are legible in five seconds: promote the eyebrow to a real styled kicker (uppercase, ~11-12px, tracked, emerald-tinted) and make the H1 name what BioStack IS for whom, demoting the current three-clause triad to the subhead — must stay descriptive, never prescriptive, per the Guidance Content Contract.
2. Collapse the hero to three genuinely distinct destinations (/start, /tools/analyzer, /providers) by deleting one of the two analyzer cards, and strip "Operator required" / "Operator access" out of the hero entirely — gating belongs at the destination or next to a price, not in the first impression.
3. Declare one primary action and enforce it at every breakpoint: make /start the single solid-fill CTA on the page, restyle the proof-section "Analyze a stack" as secondary, and cut the mobile sticky bar from four equal buttons to two with Start primary.
4. Add one credibility section between the proof section and the footer, built entirely from copy that already exists in marketing.ts: data-storage/privacy answer, free-vs-paid with real prices, and the spreadsheet differentiator — then move "BioStack is not a doctor" up into that block so it reads as stated principle rather than terminal fine print.
5. Run the a11y sweep as a single mechanical pass: raise text-white/35 and /42 to >=white/55 and footer to white/60, add a skip link, add focus-visible rings to every interactive control, complete or remove the ARIA tab pattern, add a pause affordance or drop the auto-rotation, and ship og:image with twitter card summary_large_image.

PRESERVE
- The "See what BioStack catches" heading plus StackIntelligencePanel — the single strongest asset on the page; it demonstrates rather than claims, and the BPC-157 + TB-500 overlap example is concrete enough to be credible to the actual target user.
- The dark #0B0F14 system, ambient blooms, and gradient-hairline/glass surface craft — disciplined, cohesive, and genuinely distinctive against generic SaaS; this is the part that reads premium.
- The "BioStack is not a doctor" copy itself — precise, non-defensive, and exactly the right positioning for a regulated-adjacent category; only its placement is wrong.
- The reduced-motion handling in globals.css — comprehensive and correctly non-negotiable; do not let a redesign erode it.
