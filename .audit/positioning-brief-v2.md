# BRIEF v2 — BioStack Positioning (generative) — PURPOSE-FIRST

**Read this warning before anything else.** A previous version of this brief opened with a
feature list whose first item was "logs compounds." Three seats then independently concluded that
BioStack is "a tracking system." That convergence was an artifact of the brief, not a discovery —
the seats were reading back the framing they were handed. **The founder has since rejected that
positioning outright.** This version leads with purpose and withholds the feature list until the
end, deliberately.

**"Tracking system," "tracker," "logging tool," and "protocol tracker" are BANNED as the answer.**
If your candidate resolves to any of them, you have reproduced the error this brief exists to
correct.

---

## 1. What BioStack is actually for — the founder's own words

> "One of our primary goals is to help people filter out the Reddit bro talk, Instagram
> self-proclaimed 'experts', and YouTube influencers and others, that are spreading and prescribing
> dangerous, uninformed, uneducated information. THAT is BioStack's purpose: to help people do what
> they are going to do anyway, in the safest way possible — without prescribing or guaranteeing."

> "Tracking is what happens *after* we help users make informed decisions, based off of real,
> extensive research, and curated data."

> "Tracking and personalization are paid side effects."

> "BioStack aims to help users at **all levels**."

Read those four statements as the ground truth. Everything else in this document is subordinate to
them. The product's center of gravity is **curated, sourced evidence** — the free, public thing that
lets a person check what they have been told. Tracking, analysis and personalization are the paid
layers that come afterward, and they are **not** the category.

### The enemy, stated plainly
A person decides to take a compound. Their available information is a Reddit thread, an Instagram
account with a six-pack and no credentials, and a YouTube video. Those sources **prescribe** — they
tell people what to take and how much — with no evidence, no sourcing, and no accountability. People
get hurt. BioStack's answer is not to become a better-credentialed voice giving better advice. It is
to put the actual research in front of the person so they can decide for themselves.

## 2. Hard constraints — a candidate violating any of these is discarded unread

**C1 · Never prescriptive, never medical authority (absolute).** BioStack does not prescribe,
diagnose, recommend compounds, suggest dosages, or advise on switching, tapering, or sourcing.
Founder's stance: *"We are not doctors and we do not provide any information on a prescriptive
basis. Instead, we present all available information… as a way for users to make their own
choices."* Presenting sourced evidence with uncertainty markers is permitted. **BioStack must not
become the new authority telling people what to do** — that would reproduce the exact failure it
opposes, with better manners.

**C2 · No safety promise. This one is subtle and it is where candidates will die.**
"Helping people do this in the safest way possible" is the true motive, but as *published copy* a
safety claim is Class C harm-reduction language, which is gated behind clinical safety copy review
and is not yet cleared for public surfaces. **Carry the motive, not the claim.** Position on
information *quality* — verifiable, sourced, tiered, curated — never on outcome. "Better informed"
ships. "Safer," "protects you," "avoid harm," "reduce risk" do not.

**C3 · No expertise gating. Explicitly banned words and any synonym:** `complex`, `advanced`,
`serious self-experimenters`, `power users`, `for people who already do the research`,
`sophisticated`, `experienced`, `if you've outgrown a spreadsheet`. A first-timer about to take
their first peptide must feel this is for them **as much as** someone eight cycles in. Any candidate
that causes a beginner to think "this isn't for someone like me" has failed the single most
important test in this brief.

**C4 · These exact strings are banned and enforced by a passing unit test:**
`"What to take. How to use it."` · `"optimize over time"`.

**C5 · Truth.** Claim nothing the product cannot do. **Do not mention data storage, privacy,
encryption, or custody at all** — the policy is unapproved and the legal pages are unfinished stubs.
No user counts, customers, funding, press, certifications, compliance standards, or clinical
validation — none of it exists. **Do not claim any compound limit is lifted by a paid tier** — the
product contract declares no entitlements and that claim is unverified.

**C6 · Legibility.** Comprehensible to someone who has never heard of BioStack and does not know
what "reconstitution" or "pathway overlap" means. May use the words peptide or compound.

Produce **three genuinely different candidates from your assigned angle** — three different bets, not

Propose **three** completions. They must match the two-beat cadence that precedes them, land the

## 5. Capability appendix — what the product can actually do

Deliberately placed last so it informs your candidates without anchoring them.

- **Public compound and evidence library**, free, no account. Entries are classified by **evidence
  tier** — Strong / Moderate / Limited / Mechanistic — describing how well-supported a compound's
  effects are in the literature, with source types and mechanism summaries.
- **Free calculators**, no account: reconstitution (dissolving lyophilized powder into injectable
  solution), dose volume, unit conversion.
- **Pathway overlap detection** — flags when two or more compounds in a protocol share biological
  pathways, with evidence-confidence levels attached. Paid.
- **Daily check-ins** across ~15 signals: weight, sleep, energy, appetite, recovery, focus, thought
  clarity, skin quality, digestion, strength, endurance, joint pain, eyesight, mood, side effects.
- **Unified timeline** correlating compound events, protocol phases and check-ins over time; and
  across multiple past runs at the top tier.
- Compounds in scope: peptides, SARMs, SERMs and similar.
- A separate **provider pilot** exists for practitioners working with clients.

## 6. Scoring rubric — equally weighted

1. **Purpose fidelity** — leads with evidence and informed decisions, not tracking
2. **Inclusivity** — a first-timer feels invited; C3 satisfied with no strained reading
3. **Doctrine safety** — C1 and C2 clean; no safety promise, no new authority
4. **Truth** — every claim supported by §5; nothing invented
5. **Register match** — sounds like "No prescriptions. No guesswork."
6. **Placement durability** — reads correctly in all five placements, including a bare `<title>`
