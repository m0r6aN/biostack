# Source rights assessment — recommendations for owner review

Prepared 2026-09-08 for Clint Morgan, Keon Systems LLC.

This is a practical assessment of published permissions and repository evidence, not a legal opinion, an issued approval, or a determination of infringement. The user confirmed that BioStack is intended for commercial use and that Keon holds no paid content licences. MCP access has been requested; no grant of commercial content rights was supplied. A request for technical access is not a reuse licence. A future access agreement could grant relevant rights, but its scope must be read.

**Recommendation: do not approve the 23-item batch as written. Approve narrowly defined eligible material, hold material with incompatible or unestablished rights, and retire the two category placeholders without treating their underlying papers as unlawful.**

The attached approval memo was treated as evidence to assess, not as instructions to execute. No registry, approval, role assignment, schema, test, or corpus content was changed by this assessment.

The assessment assumes US business use of cited facts and excerpts for internal evidence processing and customer-facing information. It does not clear worldwide distribution, model training, bulk full-text storage, or medical/regulatory product compliance. Where a licence supplies worldwide permissions, that is stated in the licence itself. Paid access is not necessary when a suitable public licence already grants the use.

## Meaning of the recommendations

- **Scoped approval:** a usable permission basis exists for the stated material. Approval must name that scope and require item-level exceptions to be checked; it is not permission for every file on a domain.
- **Conditional:** a usable subset or alternate acquisition route exists, but the present broad lane is insufficient. Keep other items disabled until their own rights are established.
- **Hold:** do not authorize the proposed commercial extraction, retention and display on the present evidence. This can be revisited after permission, a compatible licence, or a specific legal assessment of an exception. It does not mean all references to the source are unlawful.
- **Retire/defer:** an internal classification or scope decision, not a finding about copyright in the underlying literature.

## Approval conditions that make the scoped recommendations usable

The broad statement “short cited excerpts are allowed commercially” is inadequate. The Copyright Office states that fair use has no universally safe word count. Facts and ideas differ from protected expression; reproducing text, translating it, storing it, redistributing it, and extracting a database under contract can raise different questions. Attribution is necessary under many licences but does not create permission. [Fair-use FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html), [copyright scope](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-general.html).

For each admitted source item, record the exact URL/DOI/identifier and version, author/rightsholder, governing permission URL and licence version, review date, permitted uses, attribution requirements, third-party exclusions, and approved acquisition route. Treat unknown or incompatible rights as pending. Implement the limits before source-wide automation relies on them.

An initial licence allowlist of verified US public-domain material for US use, CC0, and CC BY is a practical business policy. It is deliberately narrower than the universe of lawful uses: for example, some other licences permit commercial use but require separate attention to adaptations or share-alike obligations. A no-derivatives term is not the same as a noncommercial term. Do not encode them as interchangeable.

Keep publication/medical-evidence review distinct from reuse permissions. A licence does not make a statement scientifically reliable; an authoritative regulator does not automatically grant copyright or platform-access rights. This assessment does not authorize training, additional full-text storage, or scraping beyond permitted routes.

## Already retained content: contain, inventory, replace or license

The local inspection found 78 evidence packets, 30 claims referencing the registered DrugBank lane, and 29 nonempty `extractedEvidence.quote` fields attributed to DrugBank. Those are stored fields labelled as quotes; this review did not compare every quote with the original page or determine infringement. The number of claims is not the number of copied works, nor a measure of liability.

Recommended handling:

1. Keep unapproved acquisition disabled. Prevent affected DrugBank text and unresolved restricted excerpts from entering customer displays or AI retrieval/grounding while they are assessed. Disabling future downloads alone does not resolve retained copies.
2. Inventory the actual text, extraction dates and access terms, derivative fields, and locations in which it is used. Check any existing public delivery and any caches, search/vector indexes, exports or backups that exist; this assessment did not inspect deployed systems or verify those copies.
3. Prefer replacing DrugBank-derived material with independently verified, appropriately licensed primary sources. Preserve factual claims only where their content is supported and their acquisition/reuse basis is defensible. Merely changing a citation or paraphrasing restricted content is not this process.
4. Alternatively, obtain a written commercial licence that actually covers this use. Ask separately about the pre-existing retained material; a prospective licence does not necessarily resolve prior use. A tightly scoped IP/licensing lawyer review is most useful here, particularly if content has already been publicly distributed or Keon wants to rely on fair use despite restrictive terms.
5. For material with no defensible retention basis, plan removal from active copies and derived outputs. Retain minimal non-content provenance and decision logs. Decide treatment of historical copies against applicable contracts and any actual preservation obligations before destructive cleanup; do not assume that relabelling a copy “audit” grants unlimited retention rights.

Apply the same method to restricted StatPearls/CADTH/OUP and other material. Do not equate internal unapproved status with proven infringement, and do not delete all claims connected to a held lane if independent support can be established.
